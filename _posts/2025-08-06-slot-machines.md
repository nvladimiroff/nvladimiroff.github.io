title: The slot machine-ification of programming
---

I tried vibe coding for the first time. I gave Anthropic some money and got myself a pro subscription to Claude, and then I gave it a pretty tough problem to chew through--a Game Boy emulator. Claude and I talked through a plan and then I let it go off and generate a ton of code. We had a lot of back and forth, but it didn't quite manage to get one working. But it got farther than I ever have! And the process was pretty fun! It was exciting to see what Claude would come up with and if it would work. It was fun and exciting and I kind of hate everything about it.

Let's talk about vibe coding.

I'll spare you the dehumanizing comparisons of vibe coding to junior devs and instead give you a new one: it's a slot machine. You give it a lever-shaped prompt and then it spins. Sometimes it works and you win, and sometimes it doesn't and you have to pull the lever again. Our brains are pretty hard-wired to love this kind of random reward dispersement.

But this just isn't the programming I know and love. I had to do some thinking, but not all that much. I didn't learn about the Game Boy's CPU, I didn't learn the ins and outs of the PPU, I didn't even learn how to write complex code better.

For me, programming is a loop of:
 * understanding something,
 * using that understanding to do something,
 * then trying to clearly communicate that newfound understanding in code.

I didn't do any of that here. If you vibe code something, you might get the same output, but the journey just isn't the same. The creativity is dead. Your brain still makes plenty of dopamine, but that dopamine comes from just pulling the lever. Not from programming as I know it.

## Does this matter?

You might say "but this doesn't matter as long as you get the product built." And from a business perspective, you're right! But programming is so much more than building sources of revenue for businesses. It's a _craft_ with a culture and community and skill and creativity.

Maybe businesses would make more money if we all just vibe coded, but turning such a fulfilling craft into a slot machine just so maybe some line goes up a little more is, to me, a tragedy.
